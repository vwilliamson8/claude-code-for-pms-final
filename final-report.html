1 · What happened

Two changes shipped together, with no off switch

On 12 August, 4.2 cut the ping wait from 90 to 60 seconds and weighted proximity up in the routing ranking. Handlers cannot change either at runtime. Misses jumped on release day and have only partly come back, while turn-downs stayed flat.

4 · The fix, shown

See it first, then decide what to change

The brief proposes three stages so that nothing changes who gets pinged until Helen has seen the evidence. The prototype below is live: switch stages in its header, send a callout, skip to expiry, click Farlight's row.

Make it visible

No routing change.

  • Roster flags "no pings in 9 days while available, 3 of last 4 missed"
  • Missed and turned-down reported separately, per responder, weekly
  • The 45 open tickets finally get an answer

Give handlers a hand

Needs a small console feed.

  • Live strip: who holds a ping, time left, outcome
  • Nudge the responder before it expires
  • "Offer to Farlight instead" on a real open callout

Change the scoring

Sign-off from Helen and input from Wen Li first.

  • A miss costs less than a decline
  • A quiet responder gets a real way back, and is told they've gone quiet
  • Changes ranking for everyone, so it goes last
  prototype.html · Farlight's ping history and the Eastgate counts are real; the callout simulation, travel times, map positions and screens are invented · open full size

Try: Stage 1, click Farlight, then switch to Kip · Eastgate. Stage 2, press Callout: routed to someone else, then Offer to Farlight instead and tap the green button on the phone.

What the fix does not do

It does not revert the 60s wait. That is Decision 3 in the brief, outside its scope, because a revert alone leaves the loop that stranded four responders. Stage 2's offer-instead only helps the score if a handler assignment records a take, and that is a question for Wen Li. The success numbers in the brief are marked provisional until confirmed with Ravi and Wen Li.

5 · Keeping it from happening again

review-checklist: the same four questions, every brief

4.2 shipped changes nobody could switch off, with the headline metric unable to see the damage, and one committed item quietly dropped. The skill is a gate on briefs, so the questions get asked before the work starts, not after the tickets arrive.

Four checks, in orderowner · success measure · scope · problem first
Verdict eachPass / Partly / Fail, with the quoted line

On my own brief the skill pushed me to name myself as owner, add success measures, and move the ping-wait revert out as a separate decision. I made those edits and marked the unconfirmed numbers provisional. I have not re-run the skill since the edits.

Where it caught a real problem

Ben's brief, "A Missed Ping Isn't a No", takes a different approach (record whether pings arrive, restore 90s, reset scores once to 12 Aug). The skill flagged that the one-time reset conflicts with the brief's own 1.5x guardrail, and that it says 10 of 16 responders but lists 11.

It also passed "problem before fix" even though the claim that pings are not reaching phones is an inference; I could not find the delivery path in the code. That is the skill's limit, noted below.

What the skill can and cannot do

Cannot: it reads only the brief. It will not read the code, so it would not have found that a miss and a decline share one function. A brief can pass all four checks and still be wrong. "Ready" means complete, not correct.

Can: make the gaps that let 4.2 through (no owner, no measure that could fail, silent scope drift, fix before problem) visible every time, in the same format, without me re-explaining it. A scheduled run, review-checklist-monday, checks 05-super-speed/brief.md at about 9:11 every Monday, but only while the app is open.
